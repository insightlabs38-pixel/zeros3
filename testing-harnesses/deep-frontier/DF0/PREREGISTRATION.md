# DF0 preregistration

Baseline `f02fa499fc88f273ce2455e41712cc484afe86c0` on research/next.
The experiment measures the current formats and cost model. The compiler,
candidate selection and defaults are fixed throughout evaluation.

## Competing hypotheses

1. GF7's savings persist when the exact source tokens still align across
   upstream releases with different codebooks. Some independent pairs
   should show a signed operating point that neither CDC, the archive,
   nor GF2 dominates after charging both retained revisions and metadata.
2. The gain is mostly a feature of the old TypeScript sequence. Sparse
   candidate runs or high raw-block fallback cause other pairs to lose
   after source, index, and recipe are charged. Even with strong token
   alignment, bit/Huffman execution or storage and verification dispatch
   may erase the advantage under concurrent whole or medium-range reads.
3. Replay may be compute-bound under warmed reads and storage-bound after
   targeted eviction or concurrent maintenance. Test both against a competing
   explanation of foreground scheduling and lock wait. Read-byte budgets
   alone are not an admission rule.

## Input partitions

- Inherited regression. GF0's TypeScript and urllib3 plus adjacent TypeScript
  v1/v2 from GF2. All have already influenced GF7. Use the first TypeScript
  pair and generated gzip inputs to develop the measurement harness.
- Fresh validation. Official curl 8.10.0, 8.10.1, 8.11.0, 8.11.1 and Lua
  5.4.6, 5.4.7, 5.4.8 gzip release histories. Evaluate adjacent pairs
  independently. Include a materialized ordered retained sequence.
- Large scope control. Python 3.13.0 and 3.13.1 official gzip sources.
  Record current format rejection above its existing size limit and measure
  applicable controls under an explicit budget.
- Blind confirmation. Official nginx 1.27.2, 1.27.3 and 1.27.4 gzip releases.
  Pin hashes with the other inputs. Open the content for evaluation only
  after the codec, harness policy and analysis rules are frozen. Exclude this
  panel from future DF1 and DF2 development and tuning.
- Diagnostics. Payload edits, reordering, compressor-setting change,
  unrelated high entropy and concatenated gzip members. Generated cases
  probe failure modes and do not count as independent upstream releases.

## Measurement and interpretation

Use clean disposable native stores and exact input size and SHA-256 before
loading. Keep the older GF7 and GF8 harnesses for independent reproduction.
Use signed reads and checked writes over real HTTP with retained source and
target closure. Measure physical allocation, post-ingest process writes,
archive finalization, source/program/index costs, CPU and latency. Record
matching yields and unchanged compressor limits. Reopen store-local cache
cold and isolate file page cache only where targeted eviction is effective.
Report cold and warm definitions. Preserve trial order and raw samples.

Use repeated opposite or seeded orders, two workers as the comparable
baseline, and sweep 1, 2, 8 and 32 clients on a bounded panel. Include
4 KiB, 64 KiB, whole, HEAD, signed PUT, and a concurrent maintenance
control. Make whole-read sample-size and uncertainty explicit. Report
per-history feasible frontier with comparable lanes and charge retentions.
Record all dominance reversals and excluded cases. Use separate operation
evidence and profiling so diagnostics cannot silently alter timed values.

Compare Huffman decode, output code emission, CAS and SHA verification,
and lock and filesystem wait. Count runs, decoded tokens, re-emitted bits,
source reads and index/program costs. Keep default paths and authority
invariants. Confirm old-reader refusal, cold reopen, corrupt/missing data
failure and scoped recovery. Attempt generic repair on the disposable store
and record its behavior for DF5. Exit with a measured bottleneck and a
proposed hypothesis for DF1. DF0 is the only authorized batch.
