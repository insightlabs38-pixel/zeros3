**Short answer.** A small, causal, explainable planner (per-lineage Beta continuation + class statistics + priors calibrated on *other* format families, expected-value investment with a premium cap) beats every Z3-36 policy on held-out families (0.696 vs 0.753 mean cost/CDC for Z3-36 expected value, book A; 0.853 vs 0.898, book B) and cuts first-sight lock-in, but it fails the preregistered promotion rule: +3.3% over the best simple baseline per fold (<5%), 3/5 folds under the I/O-heavy book, and one fold (managed/CLR+DEX+LLVM) 8.7% *worse than CDC*. The sophisticated combined planner (deferral + group re-rooting + read/tier + lifetime) is strictly worse than its own simple core: its 200 ms synchronous-analysis budget forces everything into background archival that pays double storage plus I/O. Complexity was not justified; the group re-rooter works as a lock-in repair but is not the cost winner.

## 1. Causal decision and event contract
Typed events `Arrive/Delete/Read/Tier/Tick` (chronological, strictly increasing seq, duplicate/out-of-order arrivals rejected). A policy sees only `Env`: static scheme capabilities (format sniff + earlier-measured analysis/decode rates, amplification, depth caps), the store state of already-arrived objects, charged cheap probes, charged analyses, observed reads/ages/tier, key hints (possibly wrong) and its own persistent state. Runner-side lineage/stage ground truth is unreachable (`ErrFuture` for unarrived objects). A candidate can be stored only if it was analysed (CPU charged, per-PUT or per-window budget enforced; else fall back to CDC, counted), validated (`Cand.Validate`, size-consistent components, complete closure) and `Ready` — B6 Parquet-Brotli, B4 DWARF and B3 ORC exist only as non-ready `opportunities` (loader rejects ready ones). Decisions: admission (`Store/sidecar/Defer`), maintenance (`Migrate/Flatten/Revert/Reopt/Enqueue/DropSide`) executed by the runner with budgets, capacity (migration peak), failure injection and fail-closed checks. Deferred worker: queue with priorities, TTL expiry, cancellation on delete, budget exhaustion keeps the task.
**Tests (all pass, also `-race`):** future-invariance — for 14 policies and 7 cut points, replacing every future event *and* the future numbers of every lineage leaves all decisions and the persisted policy state byte-identical (covers deferred workers/windows; non-vacuity asserted); a deliberately future-peeking policy is detected (negative control); snapshot/restore mid-run and rebuild-from-history equal the uninterrupted run (digest); corrupt snapshot rejected; ledger/store invariants over 4 retention × 14 policies with 20% injected migration failures and 90% GC (live objects always readable, no free bytes, depth/fanout caps hold, no negative costs); malformed/duplicate/not-ready series rejected; `FuzzLoadSeries` 920,567 execs clean; oracle == independent brute force and == Z3-36's exact B&B on 10 frontier cases.

## 8. Bounded analysis and deferred scheduling (H: COMPLETE)
Analysis is expensive: synchronous full analysis of every candidate costs 318 s CPU per fold-world (43 objects, ~7 s/object, B5 10.5 MiB/s, B4 2.5 MiB/s) — not PUT-path viable. `combined` sweeps (A, cost/CDC; S2-style, all folds): analyze on PUT 0.850 (1,200 s sync CPU over the 5 folds, 11 bad lineages); PUT≤1000 ms 0.974; ≤200 ms 0.981; ≤20 ms 0.982; all deferred 0.982; window CPU 10 s 1.009; hard 50 ms PUT cap without deferral 0.935 (1 bad); probe recall .5/.75/1.0 → .948/.976/.972; no probe .999 (probe quality is not the bottleneck). Deferral keeps every unprofitable lineage safe (1 bad lineage vs 11) but forfeits most of the saving: CDC bytes are written first, rep later, and migration reads/writes are paid; deferred archival (priced per lineage) only breaks even (deferred 1.035/1.036). Cancellation, TTL expiry, queue caps and budget exhaustion are exercised and tested; no production worker.

## 10. Offline bounds
Static clairvoyant plan oracle (arrival-time forms, no migration), exhaustive for ≤120k plans, bounded descent otherwise (labelled upper bound; S2 uses 300 plans), validated by brute force and Z3-36 B&B. It is a lower bound for final bytes; for dollars a hot→cold migration policy can in principle beat it. Absolute outcomes (vs always-CDC) are in every table; always-CDC itself is 66.8% above the hindsight optimum on the Z4 histories (15.95% on the Z3-36 frontier).
