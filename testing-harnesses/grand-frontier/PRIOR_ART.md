# Grand Frontier prior art

Published numbers describe the cited workloads. ZeroS3 comparisons use
matched bytes, verification and read contracts on the fixtures in this tree.

| Work and published mechanism | Incumbent ZeroS3 overlap | Distinguishing experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hierarchical similarity features with multiple sensitivities and a false-positive filter. The backup study reports 7.3% better reduction over N-Transform and Odess with up to 7.7% throughput penalty. [Palantir paper](https://henryhxu.github.io/share/hongming-asplos24.pdf) | B7 region fingerprints and C19 candidate refinement | GF3 count exhaustive candidate recall and discovery CPU, then test whether extra candidates win after closure and placement. |
| Multi-reference chunks recover boundary-shift matches. Rebase simplifies restore paths and aging releases rows. Four backup traces yielded 1.05–2.40× the one-to-one compression ratio. [SuperDelta abstract](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10533755) | FB-14 multi-base XD and release through GC | GF3 compare current XD with mined phrases and placement-aware rewrites under the same prune and read limits. |
| Locality-guided prefetch, cache-aware admission and inverse deltas support inline backup. The study reports up to 3.57× restore performance. [LoopDelta author summary](https://mingzhe-zhang.github.io/publications/tos2025) | Reconstruction layout, oldest/newest order and calibrated read costs | GF1 and GF3 compare physical packing and role selection over read traces and retained versions, including a deliberately pruned parent. |
| Inline and offline bidirectional delta preserves base locality in SGX. Evaluation compares restore overhead and space against shielded and plaintext baselines. [ShieldReduce paper page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc25/presentation/yang-jingyuan) | Native verified dependency graph and fast base access | GF3 measure co-location, actual retained packs and the locality benefit of separate directions using the same exact bytes. |
| Family clustering, tensor dedup and entropy-coded bitwise XOR. The model hub study reports 54% less storage. [ZipLLM paper page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi26/presentation/wang-zirui) | C14 typed splits and FB-14 structural matching | GF5 compare bounded sparse exact tensor reads across true lineage, reordered shards, layouts and unrelated dtypes. |
| Integer reinterpretation and entropy-coded differences against a pretrained model. The study reports around 50% mean savings. [FM-Delta proceedings](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/7b75a7339dfb256ee4b4bec028a6890b-Abstract-Conference.html) | Byte-exact XOR and structured controls | GF5 include integer residual and bitwise XOR comparators alongside the final packed, exact container cost. |
| Reuses storage metadata to avoid computing identical chunk digests at each sync phase. The implementations extend rsync and dsync. [SkySync paper page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast26/presentation/zhang-zhihao) | FB-15 reconstructable bases identified with native metadata | GF6 measure joint alternative selection when several requested files share receiver-present assets across jobs. |
| Decouples chunking and checksumming, and overlaps matching, absolute-offset reconstruction, disk and network. Evaluated over both LAN and WAN. [ParaSync paper page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast26/presentation/zhang-zhihao-parasync) | FB-15 prepared coded transfer, reusable exact decode | GF6 isolate scheduling overlap from selected wire bytes and receiver reconstruction under change and retries. |
| Hardware-plus-software compression and compression-aware scheduling in shared database storage. Deployed evaluation reports 3.55 compression ratio. [PolarStore paper page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast26/presentation/hu) | C14 SQLite/page candidates and background maintenance throttles | GF4 compare bounded policy against static strategies under changing per-namespace load with software-only accounting. |
| Configurable reversible transforms and entropy coding use field and column structure. [OpenZL release and examples](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/10/06/developer-tools/openzl-open-source-format-aware-compression-framework/) | B8/B10 codec replay and C14 typed transforms | GF2 and GF7 include exact original compressed output, independently checked checkpoints, and full cost of side information. |

GF2 also uses the published DEFLATE and gzip wire contracts for bit order,
Huffman tables, padding, trailers and CRC. Useful precedent for the
correction approach includes a real lossless practical control that encodes
generator choices alongside inflated bytes. [RFC 1951](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1951) · [RFC 1952](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1952) · [Preflate source](https://github.com/deus-libri/preflate)
